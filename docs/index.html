Deep learning term project

Can a 4-sensor headband tell resting from working?

Each record on this page is one minute of one activity, recorded with a consumer Muse S EEG headband. The minute is either rest (watching a relaxation video) or working (mental arithmetic, an n-back memory task, a Stroop colour-word task or sudoku). Four models trained on 8 other people (three read the brain-wave signal, one reads only head movement) say how likely it is that the minute is "working". Switch the question to easy vs hard to see models of the same kinds try to tell the easy level of a task from the hard level.

Data: the public UNIVERSE dataset (Anders et al., 2023). Shown here: 4 people the models never trained on. UN_109 and UN_110 were used to pick the model (validation), and UN_111 and UN_112 are the final test.

Two ways into the same signal

The deep-learning models read the brain wave itself and learn their own filters. The simple model reads 20 hand-made numbers. Head motion reads no EEG at all; it is the control.

Why band power at all? Brain rhythms are usually described by speed: slow delta and theta, alpha (usually strongest when relaxed, especially with eyes closed), faster beta and gamma. Turning 4 seconds of wave into "how much of each speed" is the classic hand-made EEG feature. The deep models skip that step: they are shown the wave itself and learn what to look for.

Scoreboard

Computed in this page from the per-minute files. Balanced accuracy is the average of the two "% called correctly" numbers (rest and working, or easy and hard), so a model that always gives the same answer scores 50%. On rest vs working that matters: 94% of test minutes are working.

How to read this

What these numbers can and cannot show.

It is rest vs working, nothing finer

The models separate "watching a relaxation video" from "doing a task". They do not tell how hard the task is: trained for easy vs hard, LaBraM scored 57.7% balanced accuracy on the test people and EEGNet, the simple model and head motion scored 49.6–51.3%, all close to a coin flip. Switch the question to easy vs hard to see it minute by minute.

Head motion is a strong rival

In our larger check (18 people, each scored by a model that never saw them), head motion alone from the headband's gyroscope and accelerometer scored AUC 0.85, above every EEG model we tried (best 0.78). On this page's own split, the head-motion-only line scores on the test people, close to the EEG models. People move differently when they rest and when they work, so some of what the EEG models pick up may be movement.

Calibration comes from the same video

Each session is calibrated on the first half of its relaxation video, and the rest minutes that get scored are the second half of that same video. Those rest minutes are therefore very close to the calibration. When that larger check calibrated on the other day's video instead, a LaBraM fine-tune scored AUC 0.73 (balanced accuracy 0.61), and same-video calibration had raised balanced accuracy for the band-power model from 0.63 to 0.69.

Tiny test set, checked more than once

The final test has 2 people, only 14 rest minutes (against 234 working minutes) and no Lab2 for UN_112. One rest minute moves UN_112's rest score by 25 points. The test people were scored more than once while models were being compared, so the test numbers are a little optimistic.

Rest always comes first

In every session shown here, the relaxation video is the first recording and the tasks follow over about two hours. So "rest" also means "start of the session". In that larger check, questionnaire periods drifted from rest-like early in the session toward task-like late in it, so part of the signal tracks time in the session. For this reason the quiz hides when each minute happened.

Which numbers run where

Simple model: recomputed live in your browser from the minute's 15 four-second band-power snapshots and the model's saved weights. LaBraM (a 3-layer cut of a pretrained EEG transformer), EEGNet (EEGNet-8,2, a compact CNN on the raw 4-channel signal, retrained for rest vs working after our easy-vs-hard EEGNet stayed at chance) and head motion: computed beforehand on the course cluster and stored. All models were trained on UNIVERSE people UN_101 to UN_108.
